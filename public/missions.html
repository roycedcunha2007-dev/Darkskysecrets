05 / A BRIEF HISTORY OF LEAVING HOME

First we lost the sky. Then we left.

From the night London’s glow blinded the world’s most famous observatory to telescopes parked a million miles out — the whole escape, on one line.

1948

  1. 1948

    London’s glow closes Greenwich

    The Royal Observatory — the place the world sets its clocks by — can no longer see the stars through the city’s light. Astronomy begins its escape.

  2. 1957

    Sputnik 1

    A polished metal sphere beeps from orbit. The Space Age begins — and the whole world listens in.

  3. 1961

    Yuri Gagarin

    The first human sees Earth from space and calls it blue. He is 27 years old.

  4. 1969

    Apollo 11

    Six hundred million people watch two humans walk on the Moon — the furthest footprints ever made.

  5. 1977

    The Voyagers

    Two probes leave with golden records of Earth’s music. Both are still flying, still calling home.

  6. 1990

    Hubble & the Pale Blue Dot

    Hubble opens its eye; Voyager 1 turns back and photographs Earth as a single pixel of light.

  7. 2000

    Humans move in

    The ISS takes its first long-term crew. No one has been off-world-less since — 26 years of someone always up there.

  8. 2004

    Cassini reaches Saturn

    Thirteen years among the rings. Its moon-lander Huygens touches down on Titan — the most distant landing ever.

  9. 2012

    Curiosity sticks the landing

    A rover the size of a car is lowered onto Mars by a rocket-powered sky crane. It finds an ancient riverbed.

  10. 2015

    Pluto, up close

    New Horizons crosses the whole solar system in nine years and finds a heart-shaped glacier waiting.

  11. 2019

    Chandrayaan-2

    India’s orbiter begins mapping the Moon in detail — groundwork for Chandrayaan-3’s historic south-pole landing.

  12. 2021

    James Webb unfolds

    The largest space telescope ever built opens like origami, a million miles out, and sees the first galaxies.

  13. NOW

    Your turn to look up

    Every one of these machines was built by people who could still see the stars. Keep the sky dark, and the next one might be yours.

Curiosity, given form — and pointed at the dark.
