01 / COSMIC OBJECTS

What fills the dark

Zoom out from a single star to the largest structures that exist. Everything here is invisible from a Bortle 9 city — and ordinary from a dark sky.

TYPICAL SCALE · 1.4 MILLION KM

Stars

Colossal spheres of plasma, fusing hydrogen into starlight for billions of years.

  • Held together by their own gravity against the pressure of fusion
  • Forge the heavy elements that make up planets — and you
  • Our Sun is an average yellow dwarf, halfway through its life

TYPICAL SCALE · 100 LIGHT-YEARS

Nebulae

Vast clouds of gas and dust — the nurseries where new stars ignite.

  • Lit from within by the radiation of the stars forming inside them
  • Some are the glowing wreckage of stars that already died
  • The Orion Nebula is faintly visible to the naked eye — under a dark sky

EVENT HORIZON · UP TO BILLIONS OF KM

Black Holes

Gravity so extreme that not even light can climb back out.

  • Born when the most massive stars collapse under their own weight
  • Superheated matter spirals in, blazing as an accretion disk
  • A supermassive one anchors the centre of nearly every galaxy

TYPICAL SCALE · 100,000 LIGHT-YEARS

Galaxies

Islands of hundreds of billions of stars, turning slowly through the dark.

  • Come as majestic spirals, smooth ellipticals and chaotic irregulars
  • Our Milky Way holds 200+ billion stars and countless worlds
  • Andromeda, our nearest large neighbour, is on a collision course with us

LARGEST SCALE · 100+ MILLION LIGHT-YEARS

The Cosmic Web

On the grandest scale, galaxies string together into a glowing web of filaments.

  • Superclusters of thousands of galaxies sit at the web’s bright knots
  • Between them lie vast, near-empty voids hundreds of millions of light-years across
  • Our galaxy belongs to Laniakea — a supercluster of 100,000 galaxies
